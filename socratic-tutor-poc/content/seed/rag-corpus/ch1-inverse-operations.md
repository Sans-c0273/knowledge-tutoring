# Chapter 1 — Inverse Operations (synthetic textbook, "Algebra Basics", p. 12–15)

<!-- chunk: c1-01 | topic: Inverse Operations | source: Algebra Basics Ch.1 p.12 | lang: en -->
An inverse operation undoes another operation. Addition and subtraction are inverse operations: if you add 5 and then subtract 5, you return to where you started. Multiplication and division are likewise inverses of each other.

<!-- chunk: c1-02 | topic: Inverse Operations | source: Algebra Basics Ch.1 p.13 | lang: en -->
To isolate a variable in an equation, apply the inverse of the operation attached to it. If the equation says x + 5 = 12, the +5 is undone by subtracting 5. The key rule: whatever you do to one side, you must do to the other, to keep both sides equal.

<!-- chunk: c1-05 | topic: Equation Balance | source: Algebra Basics Ch.1 p.15 | lang: en -->
Think of an equation as a balanced scale. The equals sign is the pivot. Removing 3 units from the left pan without removing 3 from the right tips the scale — the equality breaks. Every legal move in equation solving preserves the balance.
